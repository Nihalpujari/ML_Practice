AI for Sustainability and Climate Action

Artificial intelligence is becoming an important tool in the fight against climate change and in the
broader effort to build a more sustainable world. By analysing huge amounts of environmental data,
AI can find patterns that humans would miss and help decision makers act faster.

In energy, machine learning is used to forecast electricity demand and to predict how much power wind
and solar farms will generate. Accurate forecasts let grid operators rely more on renewable energy and
less on fossil fuels. Reinforcement learning has also been used to optimise cooling in large data
centres, cutting their energy use significantly.

In agriculture, computer vision and predictive models help farmers use water and fertiliser more
efficiently. Satellite imagery combined with AI can detect deforestation, monitor crop health, and
estimate how much carbon is stored in forests. These tools support both food security and conservation.

AI also helps with monitoring and disaster response. Models can predict floods, droughts, and wildfires
earlier, giving communities more time to prepare. Computer vision detects oil spills and plastic waste
in the ocean from satellite images.

However, AI itself has an environmental cost. Training very large models consumes a lot of electricity
and produces carbon emissions. Researchers are now working on more efficient models and on measuring the
carbon footprint of AI so that the technology helps the planet more than it harms it.
