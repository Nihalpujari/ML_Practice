# Ethical Challenges in Artificial Intelligence

Artificial intelligence systems are increasingly used to make decisions that affect people's
lives, from loan approvals to medical diagnoses and hiring. This raises serious ethical questions
that researchers and companies must address.

One of the biggest challenges is bias. Machine learning models learn from historical data, and if
that data reflects past discrimination, the model can reproduce or even amplify it. For example, a
hiring model trained on past hiring decisions may unfairly disadvantage women or minority candidates.
Detecting and reducing bias is an active and difficult area of research.

A second challenge is transparency. Many modern models, especially deep neural networks, behave like
black boxes: they produce an output but cannot easily explain why. In high-stakes settings such as
healthcare or criminal justice, the inability to explain a decision is a major problem. Explainable
AI techniques try to make model reasoning more interpretable.

Privacy is another key concern. Large models are trained on huge amounts of data, sometimes including
personal information scraped from the web. There is a risk that models memorize and leak sensitive
data. Techniques like differential privacy aim to limit how much any single person's data influences
the model.

Finally, accountability matters. When an automated system makes a harmful decision, it is often
unclear who is responsible: the developer, the company deploying it, or the user. Clear governance,
auditing, and regulation are needed so that AI is deployed responsibly and people harmed by AI
decisions have meaningful recourse.
